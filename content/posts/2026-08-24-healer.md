There was a healer, in a tower.

He often went down to the town. When he saw someone in need, he offered to help. He followed through on his offers. He helped when he could; he still tried when he couldn't.

He tried not to turn people away.

Soon, the healer made himself known. Soon, people depended on him. They came to him with trouble, and the healer listened, and often that was enough. When it was not, a question or two usually did the trick.

The healer came to understand the human soul, and see things that people hid from themselves. This helped the healer, too. What he found in others, often, he unearthed within himself later. Help and help he did, and grow he did as well.

And he was loved and admired, and paid well in food and clothes and all the worldly things.

---

There was a healer, in his tower.

People came, for his fame, his ear, his mind, his soul.

He offered help, to those who came, because he had to. Some people listened. Some people didn't.

When they didn't, the healer hurt. Why wouldn't they see it? He knew the problem, he'd seen it a hundredfold. But they, the people he was helping, didn't want to see. Why wouldn't they see?

When people didn't listen, the healer suffered. When they did, the healer suffered too, for there was no time to rest; it was time to take care of the next thing. He was running, sweating, in the desert, head light with lack of water... but still clear _enough_ to help.

Before, the healer healed because it was good. Now, the healer healed because he must.

Because he can, and so he should. Because it's the right thing to do.

After all, what is the value of a healer, if he doesn't heal?

Over time, his magic became weaker. His ears more muffled, his questions less pointed, but patinents still came.
So still, the healer healed.

---

There is no healer in his tower.

The people in the village said his mood was souring. That his eyes were sunk, his hair a mess, his anger quick.

They don't know.

"Maybe he's resting," they said.

"Good," they thought. "He was working too hard. He deserves a break."

"That way," they continued, "he'll come back happy, and we'll get the old healer back."

The people in the village don't understand.

---

There is a shepherd on a hill.

He used to be a healer, but couldn't handle it.

He begged for a break, and now he's got it.

But it's no use.

He searches for purpose, for fire, for that _life_ he remembers feeling.

He searches for want, of _anything_.

He is empty. Still so empty.

_Note: Dear reader, you may be thinking "holy shit this guy is entitled, and has a very high image of himself". If you are thinking that, I'll say "**Yep! That's the whole point of the story.**"_

_If I could have gotten rid of my self-image, and it was as easy as "stop having a self-image", I would have done it. But the fact is I am demonstrably good at helping in some contexts, and lots of people do come to me for help (or other things that equate to help). Over time, I have built a self-image of "someone whose worth as a human is in helping", and sometimes that image hurts me. Sometimes, I volunteer to help too eagerly, and that leaves me looking and feeling like a buffoon. But I also want to be a good and helpful person, and I sure wish someone like me came to help me with some of this stuff. It's complicated. This post is part of me processing that conundrum.
